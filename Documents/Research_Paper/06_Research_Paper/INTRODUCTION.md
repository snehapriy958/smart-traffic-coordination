# Section I: Introduction

Urban traffic congestion represents one of the most pressing socio-economic and environmental challenges confronting modern metropolitan regions. In rapidly growing urban centers such as Bengaluru, India, rapid motorization has far outpaced transportation infrastructure expansion, resulting in severe arterial bottlenecks, elevated fuel consumption, and hazardous vehicular emissions. The Hosur Road corridor, anchored by the Central Silk Board junction nexus, exemplifies these challenges: non-lane-based traffic flows, extreme vehicle heterogeneity (dominated by motorized two-wheelers and auto-rickshaws), high turning volumes, and recurrent queue spillback across closely-spaced signalized intersections.

Traditional urban traffic management relies heavily on Fixed-Time coordination or centralized adaptive heuristics (e.g., SCOOT, SCATS). While effective under predictable, lane-disciplined passenger car traffic, these systems degrade under oversaturated conditions with high vehicle heterogeneity and non-stationary demand surges. In recent years, deep reinforcement learning (DRL) and multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) have emerged as promising paradigms for autonomous, data-driven traffic signal control. However, existing MARL traffic research suffers from three critical shortcomings:
1. **Unrealistic Homogeneous Simulation Testbeds**: Most studies evaluate algorithms on idealized synthetic grid networks with homogeneous passenger cars, ignoring the vehicle mix and aggressive driving dynamics typical of developing metropolitan corridors.
2. **Lack of Runtime Safety Guarantees**: Purely exploratory RL algorithms cannot be directly deployed on physical traffic signals due to the risk of inducing hazardous clearance violations, phase flickering, or deadlock starvation.
3. **Statistical Inadequacy**: Many published benchmarks report results across 3 to 5 random seeds without paired statistical hypothesis testing, multiple-comparisons adjustments, or effect size reporting.

To bridge these gaps, this paper presents a high-fidelity cyber-physical simulation twin and benchmarking study of the Hosur Road corridor in Bengaluru.
