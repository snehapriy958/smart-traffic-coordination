# Abstract

Urban traffic congestion across high-density developing metropolitan corridors presents severe economic, environmental, and public health burdens. The Central Silk Board junction corridor on Hosur Road in Bengaluru, India, exhibits non-lane-based vehicle flows, extreme modal heterogeneity, high two-wheeler saturation (>67%), and recurrent intersection spillback. In this paper, we develop a high-fidelity cyber-physical simulation twin and benchmarking study of this corridor (306 edges, 213 junctions, 490 lanes) in Eclipse SUMO with TraCI. We formulate, implement, and benchmark four distinct signal control paradigms under identical traffic demand profiles across ten evaluation seeds ($N = 10, df = 9$): Fixed-Time Coordination (pre-timed baseline), Coordinated Adaptive Control (queue-balancing pressure heuristic), Dueling Double-DQN (value-based reinforcement learning), and Multi-Agent PPO (actor-critic policy gradients). To guarantee operational safety, all learning agents are constrained by a deterministic runtime safety shield enforcing minimum green (15 s), maximum green (60 s), yellow clearance (4 s), all-red clearance (2 s), and emergency vehicle preemption. Confirmatory benchmarking over a 900.0 s horizon demonstrates that Coordinated Adaptive control achieves the largest reduction in corridor travel time ($-44.60\%$, $49.87\text{ s}$ vs. $90.02\text{ s}$, paired $t = -59.25, p = 5.60 \times 10^{-13}, d_z = 18.74$), followed by Double-DQN ($-37.08\%$, $56.64\text{ s}$, paired $t = -45.57, p = 5.90 \times 10^{-12}, d_z = 14.41$). In contrast, Multi-Agent PPO yields only a modest, non-statistically significant travel time change ($-5.02\%$, $p_{\text{adj}} = 0.1153$). Coordinated Adaptive control also reduces cumulative queue delay by $-52.82\%$ ($5{,}836.8\text{ veh}\cdot\text{s}$ vs. $12{,}373.2\text{ veh}\cdot\text{s}$, $p = 2.65 \times 10^{-13}$), carbon dioxide emissions by $-23.14\%$ ($42.72\text{ kg}$ vs. $55.58\text{ kg}$), and petrol-equivalent fuel consumption by $-23.15\%$ ($18.44\text{ L}_{\text{petrol-eq}}$ vs. $23.99\text{ L}_{\text{petrol-eq}}$). The empirical carbon-to-fuel combustion ratio ($2.3164\text{ kg CO}_2 / \text{L}_{\text{petrol-eq}}$) matches theoretical octane stoichiometry within $0.208\%$. The simulation network represents a Bengaluru urban corridor using available road-network geometry and configured traffic demand. Empirical field calibration and independent real-world validation remain future work.
